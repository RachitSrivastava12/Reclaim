# Phase 3D V2 scheduler: design and threat model

## Threat model (written before implementation)

An owner can enroll or retry repeatedly, a Reactive delivery can be duplicated, delayed or reordered, a callback can revert or disappear, and Lasna time can lead Base time. Each can burn operator funds or cause a recovery attempt at the wrong time. Only the system contract may call `react`; the coordinator validates chain, emitter, opcode, topics and static ABI words before mutating state. Base Sablier and the V2 executor remain authoritative for ownership, maturity, fees and withdrawal. A successful callback request is only a pending attempt. Only Base executor logs can acknowledge or close it.

## Bounded scheduler design

Admit at most 64 active policies, and only stream IDs explicitly allowed by the operator. Keep one indexed heap entry per scheduled policy, ordered by next eligible time; cancellation-only policies stay dormant until a matching Sablier cancellation or owner retry. A cancellation observed before enrollment is retained per stream and matched to the enrolled recipient. Each Cron100 delivery processes at most 10 heap roots, accepts at most 3 callbacks, and tolerates at most 2 reverted callback requests. A failed submission is rescheduled after 300 seconds without increasing accepted attempts or the global accepted-submission counter. A stream has at most 5 reverted callback submissions over its lifetime; this counter survives re-enrollment. On the fifth failure, the scheduler marks the policy `Exhausted` and releases its slot. Heap operations cost at most `O(log 64)`; no historical enrollment scan occurs. A policy has at most 5 accepted callback submissions per version, 10 over its lifetime, a 10-minute callback acknowledgement timeout, and a 5-minute retry cooldown. Maturity scheduling adds a 2-minute cross-chain buffer. A missed tick merely delays work until the next delivered tick.

The owner can pause callback issuance while keeping subscriptions active. Permanent shutdown unsubscribes all installed subscriptions and cannot be reversed. The operator must monitor Lasna system reserves/debt, Base proxy debt, and the two distinct funding balances. Idle Cron100 deliveries still consume a reaction charge while subscribed; the live probe observed reserve decreases around 0.0156–0.0167 REACT per delivered reaction across block snapshots, not an itemized or guaranteed price.

## Event handling and state transitions

`setDestination` is owner-only and one-time. The constructor subscribes to Base Sablier cancellation and Lasna Cron100; destination setup adds six exact V2 executor topics. The listener verifies source chain, emitting address, topic, LOG opcode, topic shape, and static data length and integer bounds. Enrollment data is decoded as `(uint64 version, uint256 maxFeeWei, uint40 endTime, uint8 mode)`. The fee cap is mirrored for inspection. The coordinator has no public method to set a maturity deadline. `endTime` comes only from the executor's authenticated `PolicyEnrolled` log, which itself reads Sablier `getEndTime`.

An admitted enrollment starts `Dormant` for Cancellation mode, or `Scheduled` at `endTime + 120 seconds` for Maturity and Either. A matching Sablier cancellation makes Cancellation or Either eligible immediately, including when its log was observed before enrollment. A valid owner retry makes an active, nonpending, nonterminal policy `Retryable`; it cannot postpone an earlier due time, and Maturity/Either cannot run before buffered maturity unless a matching cancellation permits Either. Cron changes an accepted callback to `Pending` with an `eventId` and 600-second timeout. A reverted request increments only the persistent submission-failure counter; the first four leave the policy `Retryable` with a 300-second delay, and the fifth marks it `Exhausted` and releases capacity. Neither result completes or revokes the Base policy. No callback request is an acknowledgement. A matching `RecoverySkipped` changes transient reasons 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 10 to `Retryable` after 300 seconds. Reasons 1, 2, 3 and 6 enter `TerminalPending` with a 24-hour resolution timer; expiry releases capacity but leaves the state `TerminalPending`, never `Completed` or `Revoked`. A delayed authentic terminal event can still close it, including after permanent shutdown. Anyone may call `releaseTimedOutTerminal` when due, even if cron deliveries stop or the callback budget is exhausted. A matching `RecoverySucceeded` enters `Acknowledged` and waits for `PolicyCompleted`; a partial withdrawal can receive another bounded attempt after 600 seconds. `PolicyCompleted` and `PolicyRevoked` remove the heap entry and active slot. A same-version terminal tombstone blocks a delayed enrollment. A later enrollment version can start a new policy if allowed, within capacity, and its lifetime failure budget remains. Old or unmatched outcomes cannot change it.

The system callback configuration fixes destination chain 84532 and gas limit 600,000. Its payload is exactly `abi.encodeCall(SablierRecoveryExecutorV2.recover, (address(0), eventId, streamId, version))`; the official callback proxy is expected to inject the Reactive sender into that zero slot. The mock tests verify payload bytes but do not verify a live proxy injection.

## Admission, retry and funding policy

There are at most 64 admitted streams and 64 heap entries. The operator must call `setStreamAllowed(streamId, true)` before an enrollment event arrives; this controls only subsidy admission, and gives no power over Base assets or user fee credits. `setStreamAllowed(id, false)` prevents future admission but does not revoke an already-admitted policy. Admission is closed by default. A full queue or unallowed stream rejects new enrollment observation and records its version, so it is **not** automatically admitted when a slot later frees; the Base owner must re-enroll with a newer version after operator approval. Each version has five accepted submissions, each stream has ten accepted submissions and five failed submissions over its lifetime, and the global accepted-submission counter cannot exceed 512. The owner sets `callbackBudget` up to 512; it starts at zero, can be lowered no further than already submitted, and cannot be raised beyond 512. New stream IDs cannot bypass this global cap. Exhaustion releases the active slot while retaining version and lifetime history. A canceled stream's owner retry can request an attempt even before a cancellation is seen, but the executor will return `NotCanceled`; the same attempt and cooldown limits apply. Once lifetime accepted attempts are consumed, a later version becomes `Exhausted` at its first due tick. Once the failure limit is consumed, a later version is immediately `Exhausted` without admission.

Operator policy: fund the Lasna system reserve and the Base executor's callback reserve separately, monitor `reserves` and `debt` on Lasna plus Base proxy debt, and pause when expected spend is not funded. `setPaused` stops callback requests but leaves every subscription active, so idle Cron100 reactions continue to cost funds. `shutdownPermanently` removes the exact two constructor and six destination subscriptions (or only two if no destination was configured), stops all future callback issuance, and disallows reopening, budget edits and destination setup. Delayed terminal events may still reconcile state; other delayed events are ignored. Shutdown does not revoke Base policies or refund user fee credits: Base executor owners retain their existing withdrawal/revocation rights. `coverDebt` uses the installed Reactive payer behavior. The allowlist and global cap bound accepted callback submissions, but do not cap subscription reaction charges, failed-submission reaction gas, operator-funded Base callback costs already in flight, or third-party delivery behavior. No operator control can withdraw user assets or fee reserves.

## Gas and bounded work

Measured by `testGasEstimates` on local Foundry with a mock system contract (gas includes test delivery overhead and test allowlisting, not live Lasna billing): enrollment **269,717**, idle Cron100 **127,135**, one due Cron100 **418,073**, marginal callback submission estimate **290,938** (due minus idle), and a 10-policy due backlog Cron100 **578,716**. The 10-policy tick submits three callbacks; the rest remain queued. These are estimates rather than a live fee quote. The heap costs `O(log n)` storage reads/writes per insertion or removal; its maximum depth is six at 64 policies. A tick performs at most ten root operations and three accepted callback requests plus up to two reverted request attempts. There is no scan of historical enrollments. The `observedCron`, version, cancellation and submission-failure mappings grow over time in storage but are never enumerated in `react`.

The live probe's 0.0156–0.0167 REACT reserve decreases across sampled delivery blocks are evidence for *that probe*, not a price for this coordinator. It remains continuously subscribed, including while idle or paused, so an operator must budget for each delivered Cron100 reaction and possible system debt.

## Verification and open risks

The Foundry tests cover malformed and wrong-source logs, direct-call rejection, duplicate cron, cancellation before enrollment, modes, queue capacity and fairness, missing acknowledgements, selective callback request reverts, persistent failure exhaustion, re-enrollment across failure limits, unrelated stream progress, single capacity release, accepted-counter isolation, allowlist removal after admission, timeouts, transient skips, retry spam, revocation, same-version terminal ordering, stale outcomes, 64-policy exhaustion and capacity reuse, budget exhaustion, admission, shutdown subscription arguments, and randomized heap invariants. The integrated test records real V2 executor enrollment and recovery logs, relays them into the coordinator through a mock system, and checks the executor's actual withdrawal and completion. It mocks Reactive delivery, Cron100, the callback proxy and Sablier; it does not simulate two live chains. The existing Base Sepolia fork tests with real Sablier contracts remain unchanged.

Final local gates passed: `forge fmt --check`, `forge build`, `BASE_SEPOLIA_RPC_URL=https://sepolia.base.org forge test -vv`, and `git diff --check`. The full suite reports 107 passed, 0 failed, including both Base Sepolia fork tests and 30 scheduler tests. The three V2 scheduler files remain untracked, so `git diff --check` sees no patch; separate `git diff --no-index --check` checks emitted no whitespace diagnostics for each file. Foundry reports lint notices in existing contracts and timestamp-mutation style notices in the scheduler test. The mock gas estimates above are not deployment fee quotes.

Unverified on Lasna: live system subscription charges for eight listeners, callback acceptance and delivery timing, any implicit per-reaction gas ceiling, cross-chain clock skew beyond the 120-second buffer, and whether the 600,000 Base callback gas limit suffices for all Sablier stream variants. The Base executor checks real maturity and fee conditions, so a clock-leading request is skipped and retried within budget. Retry logs delivered before enrollment are ignored, although the later enrollment schedules maturity and matching cancellation remains recorded. An allowed stream whose enrollment event was rejected for capacity needs a newer Base enrollment after capacity frees. If no Cron100 is delivered because reserve is depleted or system debt accumulates, due work waits until delivery resumes; terminal-pending capacity can also be released by an external call after 24 hours. Timestamp arithmetic saturates at `uint40.max`; at that boundary scheduling can no longer advance. No live deployment, broadcast or wallet action was performed.
